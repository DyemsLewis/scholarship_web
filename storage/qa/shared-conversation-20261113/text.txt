 Complete Guide to the Scholarship Process in the Philippines 
 From the perspective of scholarship providers, applicants, administrators, schools, and government agencies 
 In the Philippines, a scholarship is more than simply submitting an application and receiving financial assistance. It involves an entire system of program creation, funding, eligibility screening, application processing, evaluation, approval, awarding, fund disbursement, academic monitoring, renewal, graduation, and program closure . 
 There are two main sides : 
 Scholarship providers — government agencies, local government units (LGUs), schools, foundations, companies, NGOs, and individuals who finance or administer scholarships. 
 Scholarship applicants — students who apply for financial assistance and, if approved, become scholarship recipients or scholars . 
 There are also supporting participants: parents or guardians, school registrars, accounting offices, banks, scholarship committees, auditors, and government regulators. 
 Important distinction : The Philippines does not have one universal scholarship procedure. CHED, DOST, LGUs, private foundations, and universities have different requirements, selection criteria, benefits, and renewal rules. The following explains the full scholarship lifecycle, including processes that are common across programs and additional procedures that apply only to certain providers. 
 1 . Types of scholarships in the Philippines 
 Scholarship type Typical provider Primary basis 
 Academic or merit-based Universities, foundations, LGUs Grades, ranking, academic performance 
 Financial need-based Government, NGOs, foundations Household income and financial circumstances 
 Science and technology DOST-SEI Academic qualifications, examination or approved selection pathway 
 Tertiary educational assistance CHED and other government programs Program-specific eligibility and prioritization 
 Athletic Universities, sports organizations Sports performance and academic eligibility

 A scholarship may cover tuition, allowances, books, transportation, accommodation, and other expenses. A grant-in-aid , subsidy , or student financial assistance program may have different legal and administrative conditions. 
 For example, free tuition at a qualifying state university under Republic Act No. 10931 is not the same thing as winning a competitive scholarship. 
 Part I — Complete process for scholarship providers 
 Phase 1: Planning and creating the scholarship program 
 Before accepting applications, a provider must define the program. 
 Step 1. Identify the purpose 
 The provider determines why the scholarship exists. 
 Examples include helping low-income students, rewarding academic achievement, supporting students in priority degree programs, encouraging local residents to complete college, or developing future employees. 
 The provider establishes measurable goals, such as supporting 100 students annually or achieving a target graduation rate. 
 Step 2. Identify the provider's legal and organizational structure 
 Possible structures include a government agency, LGU, school, registered nonprofit foundation, corporation, or private donor. 
 Depending on the structure, the provider may need : 
 Legal registration and appropriate organizational authority. 
 Board or management approval. 
 A designated program administrator. 
 Accounting and financial controls. 
 Data privacy policies. 
 A written scholarship agreement. 
 Government budget authorization, where applicable. 
 A private organization is not automatically required to register as a separate foundation just to award scholarships. However, its organizational structure, fundraising activities, tax treatment, and public representations may create additional requirements. 
 Step 3. Establish the scholarship policy 
 The provider creates a formal scholarship policy or program manual specifying : 
 Program name and purpose. 
 Target beneficiaries. 
 Available scholarship slots. 
 Educational levels covered. 
 Eligible schools and courses. 
 Eligibility requirements. 
 Required documents. 
 Application opening and closing dates. 
 Screening and scoring procedures. 
 Selection and approval authorities. 
 Financial benefits. 
 Payment schedules. 
 Renewal requirements. 
 Grounds for suspension or termination. 
 Appeals and reconsideration procedures. 
 Fraud prevention and audit procedures. 
 Data privacy and records retention rules. 
 Program monitoring and reporting. 
 The policy should distinguish mandatory eligibility requirements from competitive selection criteria . 
 For example, a minimum grade may be required to qualify, while higher grades may improve an applicant's ranking. 
 Step 4. Determine the scholarship budget 
 The provider calculates how much money is needed per scholar and for the entire program.

 Private providers typically secure donor commitments, board-approved allocations, or dedicated scholarship funds. 
 Providers should account for continuing scholars before promising new awards, particularly when scholarships cover multiple academic years.

 Small providers may combine roles, but sensitive decisions should have appropriate checks and approvals. 
 Phase 2: Preparing and opening applications 
 Step 7. Establish eligibility rules 
 Providers may require : 
 Filipino citizenship or another specified legal status. 
 Residency in a particular city, province, or region. 
 Enrollment or planned enrollment in an eligible school. 
 A specified year level or degree program. 
 Minimum grades or general weighted average (GWA). 
 Maximum household income. 
 No disqualifying academic or disciplinary record. 
 No conflicting scholarship, when the program prohibits simultaneous awards. 
 Successful completion of an examination, interview, or assessment. 
 Eligibility rules must follow the governing law, funding terms, and published program guidelines. 
 Step 8. Define the required documents 
 Typical documents are :

 Not every scholarship requires every document. For instance, a first-year applicant may submit Grade 12 records, while an existing college student may submit a college transcript.

 The provider establishes deadlines for :

 First disbursement. 
 Step 10. Publish the scholarship announcement 
 Announcements are commonly posted through official websites, school offices, social media pages, bulletin boards, and LGU information channels. 
 A complete announcement should include eligibility, benefits, number of slots if fixed, document requirements, submission instructions, deadlines, official contact information, and appeal procedures. 
 The provider should also publish any important limitations, such as non-stackable benefits or return-service obligations. 
 Phase 3: Receiving and processing applications 
 Step 11. Receive applications 
 Applications may be submitted through online portals, Google Forms, school scholarship offices, email, or physical submission counters. 
 Each submission should be assigned a unique application or reference number. 
 The system records the submission timestamp, academic year, scholarship program, and application status. 
 Step 12. Validate the application 
 The administrator checks whether the application was submitted on time, all mandatory fields are completed, required files are readable, and the applicant meets basic program requirements. 
 Potential outcomes : 
 Complete. 
 Incomplete. 
 Requires clarification. 
 Ineligible. 
 Duplicate submission. 
 Pending verification. 
 Incomplete applications may be returned for correction if the program allows a correction period. 
 Step 13. Verify documents and eligibility 
 Verification may involve checking records with the school registrar, confirming residency with the relevant issuing authority, validating income documentation, checking examination records, and reviewing conflicting awards. 
 Sensitive information should be collected only when necessary, with appropriate privacy notices, lawful processing grounds, access controls, and retention rules. 
 Step 14. Conduct examination, interview, or assessment 
 Depending on the scholarship, applicants may undergo written examinations, interviews, essay assessments, portfolio reviews, talent demonstrations, or financial-need evaluations. 
 Not all programs require an examination or interview. 
 Step 15. Score and rank applicants 
 A provider may use a weighted scoring system. 
 Criterion Example weight 
 Academic performance 40% 
 Financial need 35% 
 Interview 15% 
 Leadership/community involvement 10% 
 Total 100% 
 
 
 This is only an example, not a national scholarship scoring standard. 
 The provider must establish how scores are calculated, how ties are resolved, how conflicts of interest are managed, and what happens when eligible applicants exceed the available slots. 
 Step 16. Review and approve the selected applicants 
 The scholarship committee reviews results and prepares a recommendation. 
 An authorized official or board then approves the final award list. 
 Records may include the screening checklist, score sheets, committee minutes, approved recipient list, and decision documentation. 
 Step 17. Notify applicants 
 Successful applicants receive an offer or award notice. Unsuccessful applicants receive a rejection or status notice according to program policy. 
 The provider may also maintain a waitlist and set an appeal or reconsideration period. 
 Phase 4: Awarding and releasing benefits 
 Step 18. Obtain acceptance and execute the scholarship agreement 
 The recipient may be required to sign a scholarship agreement, undertaking, or contract. 
 It should explain : 
 Scholarship benefits and duration. 
 Scholar obligations. 
 Grade and enrollment requirements. 
 Renewal conditions. 
 Restrictions on other scholarships. 
 Reporting responsibilities. 
 Suspension or termination grounds. 
 Any repayment or return-service provisions. 
 Dispute resolution procedures. 
 For minors, parent or guardian participation may be required. 
 Step 19. Conduct orientation 
 The provider explains scholarship rules, grade requirements, reporting deadlines, payment schedules, allowable expenses, and how to contact administrators. 
 Step 20. Verify enrollment before payment 
 Many providers require proof that the selected scholar has actually enrolled in an eligible institution and program. 
 The provider may verify registered units, school accreditation or recognition where relevant, tuition assessment, and academic term. 
 Step 21. Process disbursement 
 Typical workflow : 
 Possible payment methods include bank transfer, direct school payment, approved electronic payment channels, or other officially authorized methods. 
 Disbursement may occur monthly, per semester, per academic year, or on another schedule defined by the scholarship. 
 Phase 5: Monitoring and renewal 
 Step 22. Monitor academic performance 
 The provider may require scholars to submit grades, enrollment certifications, academic progress reports, attendance records, or other evidence of compliance. 
 Step 23. Evaluate renewal eligibility 
 Renewal may depend on : 
 Meeting the minimum GWA. 
 Passing all required subjects. 
 Maintaining minimum academic units. 
 Remaining enrolled in an eligible program. 
 Continuing to satisfy residency or income conditions where applicable. 
 Fulfilling reporting and service obligations. 
 Having remaining scholarship eligibility. 
 A scholarship may be automatically renewable subject to conditions, or scholars may need to reapply each year. 
 Step 24. Handle academic or administrative problems 
 Possible cases include failed subjects, incomplete grades, a leave of absence, transfer of school, change of course, delayed graduation, withdrawal, disciplinary findings, or suspected fraudulent documents. 
 The provider should use its published policy to determine whether to approve an exception, impose probation, suspend benefits, terminate the award, or permit an appeal. 
 Step 25. Maintain program and financial records 
 Providers maintain application records, selection documentation, award agreements, payment transactions, academic reports, and other required evidence. 
 Government programs may be subject to Commission on Audit (COA) requirements and agency-specific accounting rules. Private programs follow their applicable corporate, accounting, tax, and donor obligations. 
 Phase 6: Graduation and closure 
 Step 26. Confirm completion 
 The provider verifies that the scholar has graduated or otherwise completed the covered educational program. 
 Step 27. Close the scholarship record 
 The administrator checks outstanding payments, final reports, pending obligations, and remaining program commitments. 
 Step 28. Enforce applicable post-graduation obligations 
 Some scholarships require return service, employment commitments, or other conditions. Others have no post-graduation service requirement. 
 These must be determined from the specific scholarship's governing terms. 
 Step 29. Evaluate program outcomes 
 Providers may measure graduation rates, retention, academic performance, total funds disbursed, employment outcomes, and beneficiary satisfaction. 
 This information can support future budgeting and program improvements. 
 Part II — Complete process for scholarship applicants 
 The applicant's process begins before submitting any documents and continues until graduation or termination of the award. 
 Phase 1: Finding and choosing scholarships 
 Step 1. Identify suitable scholarship opportunities 
 Students may search through official government websites, school scholarship offices, LGU offices, private foundations, company programs, and verified scholarship announcements. 
 Some important Philippine scholarship sources include : 
 Provider Common programs or assistance 
 Commission on Higher Education Merit scholarships, specialized scholarships, faculty development 
 Department of Science and Technology Undergraduate science and technology scholarships, junior-level and graduate scholarships 
 UniFAST Tertiary Education Subsidy (TES), Tulong Dunong Program (TDP) 
 Department of Education Basic education assistance and certain student support programs 
 Technical Education and Skills Development Authority Technical-vocational training scholarships 
 Provincial, city, or municipal LGUs Residency-based scholarship or educational assistance 
 Universities and colleges Entrance, academic, athletic, institutional, and financial-need scholarships 
 Private foundations and companies Merit, need-based, community, and industry-specific awards 
 
 
 Step 2. Read the official guidelines 
 Applicants must check whether the scholarship accepts their educational level, course, school, grades, household income, citizenship, residency, and current scholarship status. 
 They must also check whether the program is currently accepting applications. 
 Step 3. Compare the benefits and restrictions 
 Important questions include whether the scholarship covers tuition or only allowances, whether it lasts one semester or the full degree, whether renewal is guaranteed, and whether another scholarship may be held simultaneously. 
 Step 4. Check whether the applicant can apply to multiple scholarships 
 Applying to several scholarships is not automatically prohibited. However, receiving overlapping benefits may be restricted. 
 For example, the revised CHED Merit Scholarship Program guidelines exclude existing recipients of certain nationally government-funded scholarships and grants, subject to specified exceptions. 
 Applicants should disclose existing awards and follow the rules of each program. 
 Phase 2: Preparing the application 
 Step 5. Gather the documents 
 Applicants request academic records from their school, civil documents from appropriate issuing offices, and financial or residency records from relevant authorities. 
 Some documents take time to obtain, so preparation should begin before the deadline. 
 Step 6. Check document validity 
 Before submitting, verify that names, dates, school information, grades, signatures, and certifications are consistent. 
 For online submissions, files should be readable, complete, correctly oriented, and within the portal's allowed format and size. 
 Step 7. Prepare any essay, examination, or interview requirements 
 Applicants may need to explain their financial circumstances, academic goals, community contributions, or career plans. 
 Responses must be truthful and consistent with submitted documents. 
 Phase 3: Submitting the application 
 Step 8. Create an application account, if required 
 Online scholarship systems may require an email address, password, mobile number, and account verification. 
 Some programs accept paper applications instead. 
 Step 9. Complete the application form 
 Typical information includes : 
 Information category Common fields 
 Personal Full name, birthdate, sex where required, citizenship 
 Contact Address, email, mobile number 
 Family Parents or guardians, household members 
 Financial Income sources, household income, dependents 
 Educational School, year level, course, GWA 
 Scholarship Program selected, existing awards 
 Supporting Essay, certifications, uploaded documents 
 
 
 Step 10. Submit and retain proof 
 The applicant submits the completed application before the deadline and keeps the reference number, acknowledgment email, or submission receipt. 
 Submitting an application does not mean the scholarship has been approved. 
 Phase 4: Application evaluation 
 Step 11. Wait for initial screening 
 The provider checks the application against its requirements. 
 The applicant may be asked to correct or clarify documents. 
 Step 12. Complete assessments 
 If required, the applicant takes an examination, attends an interview, or completes another assessment. 
 Step 13. Wait for final selection 
 Eligible applicants may be ranked based on published criteria. Even applicants who satisfy all minimum requirements may not receive awards when slots or funds are limited. 
 Step 14. Receive the decision 
 Possible decisions include : 
 Qualified : Meets the eligibility requirements but is not necessarily selected. 
 Shortlisted : Selected for further assessment. 
 Approved or awarded : Selected to receive the scholarship, subject to any remaining conditions. 
 Waitlisted : May receive an award if a slot becomes available. 
 Incomplete : Missing or invalid requirements. 
 Disqualified : Failed an eligibility condition or violated applicable rules. 
 Not selected : Eligible or assessed but not awarded a slot. 
 The applicant should follow the official appeal procedure if they believe a factual or administrative error occurred. 
 Phase 5: Accepting the scholarship 
 Step 15. Review the award offer 
 The applicant verifies the benefit amount, duration, covered expenses, required grades, service obligations, and payment schedule. 
 Step 16. Sign the agreement 
 The applicant signs the necessary forms and provides parent or guardian authorization when applicable. 
 Step 17. Complete orientation and enrollment verification 
 The applicant may need to attend orientation, confirm their enrollment, submit a certificate of registration, and provide payment details. 
 Step 18. Receive scholarship benefits 
 The student receives the approved benefits through the provider's payment process. 
 Payment may be delayed by document deficiencies, enrollment verification, funding release schedules, banking problems, or administrative processing. 
 Phase 6: Maintaining the scholarship 
 Step 19. Continue studying and comply with scholarship rules 
 Common obligations include maintaining required grades, completing minimum units, submitting records, and notifying the provider of relevant academic changes. 
 Step 20. Submit periodic reports 
 Depending on the program, scholars may submit semester grades, certificates of enrollment, renewal forms, progress reports, or other documents. 
 Step 21. Apply for renewal or undergo continuation assessment 
 Some scholarships require a fresh application. Others require only proof of continuing eligibility. 
 Step 22. Report changes 
 Students should promptly report a transfer of school, change of degree program, leave of absence, withdrawal, delayed graduation, or other circumstances affecting eligibility. 
 Step 23. Complete the scholarship 
 At graduation or the end of the covered period, the scholar may need to submit final academic documents, complete exit procedures, or fulfill return-service obligations. 
 Part III — How actual Philippine government programs differ 
 The general lifecycle above is useful, but actual programs have specific rules. These examples demonstrate why providers and applicants must refer to the correct guidelines for the relevant academic year. 
 1 . CHED Merit Scholarship Program (CMSP) 
 The revised 2025 CHED Merit Scholarship Program guidelines provide a concrete example of a competitive government scholarship. 
 The published rules include a minimum Grade 12 GWA of 93%, a combined annual parental or guardian gross-income ceiling of ₱500,000, eligible priority degree programs, and a ranking process subject to available slots. 
 Applicant process 
 Check eligibility and priority courses, prepare the required documents, apply through the designated CHED process, undergo verification and ranking, and await official results. 
 The applicant's documents under these guidelines include the application form, birth certificate, certified Grade 12 report card, and an accepted form of household-income evidence. 
 Provider-side process : CHED or its authorized implementing offices receive applications, verify qualifications, rank candidates, approve awards, and administer benefits and continuation under program rules. 
 Note that CHED also introduced other scholarship initiatives, including the Bagong Pilipinas Merit Scholarship Program for academic year 2026–2027, with different eligibility criteria. Its published requirements included a Grade 12 GWA of at least 95% or top-five graduating-class standing and a different income ceiling. These are separate program requirements, not a replacement for every CHED scholarship.

 2 . DOST-SEI Undergraduate Scholarships 
 DOST-SEI scholarships support students pursuing approved science and technology fields. 
 The 2027 undergraduate scholarship guidance describes eligibility for qualifying graduating Grade 12 students and certain senior high school graduates who have not yet enrolled in college. Applicants use the electronic scholarship application system, complete required modules, upload documents, and follow the assessment process. 
 Applicant process 
 Eligibility check → Online registration → Required forms and uploads → Application validation → Qualifying assessment → Results → Scholarship agreement → Enrollment in an approved program → Benefits and monitoring. 
 DOST-SEI has multiple scholarship categories, including Merit and RA 7687, and requirements differ by category and application cycle. Scholars must also check the applicable service and other contractual obligations. 
 Official reference: 
 3 . UniFAST — TES and Tulong Dunong Program 
 UniFAST coordinates student financial assistance programs under the Philippine tertiary education framework. 
 Tertiary Education Subsidy (TES) 
 A grant-in-aid program for prioritized eligible tertiary students, subject to available funds. The published benefit schedule lists ₱20,000 per academic year for SUC/LUC beneficiaries and ₱27,000 for private HEI beneficiaries, with certain additional benefits for qualifying categories. 
 The published Tulong Dunong Program (TDP) information lists ₱7,500 per regular semester, subject to program eligibility, prioritization, and funding. These figures are from the official published program information and should be verified against the current implementing guidelines before applying. 
 Unlike a typical independently submitted private scholarship application, TES processing involves participating higher education institutions and program-level eligibility and prioritization procedures.

 An LGU scholarship normally follows an ordinance, resolution, approved budget, or local program guidelines. 
 The typical provider-side process is :

 An LGU may additionally require barangay residency certifications, voter-related documentation where legally appropriate, community service, or other locally authorized conditions. 
 There is no single nationwide GWA requirement, income ceiling, or benefit amount for all LGU scholarships. 
 5 . Private foundation and corporate scholarships 
 Private scholarship providers commonly use their own application forms and selection policies. 
 Their process often includes online registration, document screening, academic or financial evaluation, interviews, final committee approval, scholarship agreement, and direct payment or allowances. 
 Some corporate scholarships may offer internships, mentoring, employment opportunities, or employment-related conditions. These should be stated clearly in the award terms.

 The applicability of each law depends on the scholarship provider and program. A private donor, for example, does not automatically follow the same disbursement procedures as a national government agency. 
 Data privacy requirements 
 Scholarship applications often involve personal and sensitive information, including grades, household income, family circumstances, and government-issued records. 
 A provider should establish : 
 A clear privacy notice and lawful basis for processing. 
 Collection limited to necessary information. 
 Access restricted by staff responsibilities. 
 Secure document storage and transmission. 
 Procedures for correcting records. 
 A justified retention and deletion schedule. 
 Security incident and breach response procedures. 
 Proper arrangements when sharing information with schools, banks, or service providers. 
 Additional safeguards when applicants are minors. 
 Consent is not always the only lawful basis for processing under Philippine privacy law. The correct basis depends on the processing activity. 
 Financial accountability 
 For every payment, providers should be able to establish : 
 Who was entitled to receive the benefit. 
 What benefit was authorized. 
 Which academic period the payment covered. 
 Whether the payment was approved. 
 How and when it was released. 
 Whether it was received or credited. 
 Whether any correction, reversal, or recovery occurred. 
 The provider should reconcile the scholarship roster, approved obligations, actual disbursements, and available budget. 
 Fraud prevention 
 Common risks include falsified grades, manipulated income documents, duplicate applications, impersonation, unauthorized changes to bank details, fabricated enrollment records, and staff conflicts of interest. 
 Controls can include identity checks, independent document verification, approval separation, audit logs, secure bank-detail changes, and a confidential complaint mechanism.

 Final distinction: Who does what? 
 The applicant is responsible for finding a suitable program, proving eligibility, submitting truthful and complete information, completing assessments, accepting the award, and maintaining compliance. 
 The provider is responsible for creating a lawful and funded program, communicating transparent rules, verifying applications, selecting recipients fairly, releasing approved benefits, maintaining records, handling appeals, and monitoring program performance. 
 The school may act as a verification partner, recommending institution, processing institution, or scholarship provider, depending on the program. 
 Government agencies and regulators establish and administer their respective programs, supervise relevant institutions, and enforce applicable legal, financial, and accountability requirements. 
 The complete process is therefore not simply Apply → Approve → Receive Money . It is an ongoing administrative and financial relationship that can span multiple semesters or years.

 This guide covers the general end-to-end framework and major operational exceptions. An exhaustive legal procedure for every scholarship in the Philippines would require reviewing each program's current rules, since there is no single process that governs every provider.
